# SovLend — Bitcoin-Enabled Lending: Investor & Leadership Briefing

**Date:** 14 September 2026
**Prepared for:** Jump Start Africa investors and leadership
**Prepared by:** Oliseh Mwijusya (Genesis)

---

## The picture in one paragraph

SovLend is now live and running with Bitcoin as a real, working part of how the institution takes in investor capital and pays out loans — alongside cash, mobile money, and bank transfer. Investors fund the institution over the Lightning Network in seconds. Loans go out to clients through whichever channel fits — cash, mobile money, bank, or Bitcoin — chosen by the cashier at the counter. Every transaction, in every currency, sits on one accounting system: fully recorded, priced at the moment it happens, and never quietly edited afterward. This is the working phase of the system. It is deliberately built lean and safe first, with a clear, funded path to the fuller design — a treasury that no single person can move alone, self-service investor withdrawals, and Bitcoin savings for clients — as volume and trust grow.

---

## 1. How an investor funds SovLend today

An investor opens their portal, chooses an amount, and SovLend generates a real Bitcoin Lightning invoice through Blink, a licensed Lightning payments provider — SovLend does not run its own crypto infrastructure; it uses a proven rail built for exactly this. The investor scans the invoice and pays from any Lightning wallet. Payment is confirmed automatically, the exchange rate at that exact moment is captured and stored permanently, and the contribution appears in the investor's portfolio — typically within seconds of payment.

This is not a demo or a pilot in isolated conditions. It is running in production today.

## 2. Where that Bitcoin goes

Bitcoin received from investors flows into SovLend's own treasury account, which **Fred Mawanda manages** as the institution's designated treasury manager — topping up the operating balance so Bitcoin disbursements can be paid out to clients. The account itself belongs to and is controlled by SovLend; Fred is the accountable individual who administers it day to day. This is a deliberate, hands-on first step: one accountable manager overseeing every treasury movement while the system is proven in real use, before the fuller multi-signature treasury design (below) distributes that responsibility across several independent approvers.

## 3. How loans reach clients: four channels, one system

When a loan is approved and ready to disburse, the cashier chooses how the client actually receives the money:

- **Cash**
- **Mobile money**
- **Bank transfer**
- **Bitcoin**

Whichever channel is used, the loan itself — its terms, its interest, its repayment schedule — is identical. The channel is simply the last-mile delivery method. This gives clients real choice without fragmenting the product.

## 4. Keeping Bitcoin disbursements safe

Bitcoin moves faster and more irreversibly than mobile money, so it is held to a tighter standard from day one:

- Cashiers may release **up to $500 per day** in Bitcoin on their own authority.
- **Anything above that requires sign-off from the branch manager** before it goes out — the system itself now blocks any release beyond the cap unless a manager approves it, rather than relying on process alone.

## 5. The accounting behind it — this is the part that protects everyone

This is the most important section of this briefing, because it is what makes SovLend trustworthy with other people's money, in any currency.

- **One ledger, not two.** A Bitcoin transaction and a cash transaction post to the exact same double-entry accounting system. There is no separate "crypto ledger" to reconcile by hand — an investor or an auditor sees one complete picture.
- **Nothing is ever silently edited.** Every entry, once posted, is permanent. If a correction is needed, it happens through a new, visible offsetting entry — never by rewriting history. This is the same discipline banks and auditors expect from any regulated ledger.
- **Every price is captured and frozen at the moment of the transaction.** Whether it's an investor's contribution or a client's Bitcoin disbursement, the exact exchange rate used is stored permanently alongside it — so every number can be independently reconstructed later, not taken on trust.
- **Approvals apply the same way to every channel.** The maker-checker discipline SovLend already runs for cash and mobile-money disbursements applies identically to Bitcoin — no channel gets a shortcut around oversight.

## 6. Where this is headed next

The system today is the first, deliberately lean phase of a larger design. The roadmap ahead, in the order we intend to build it:

1. **Give investors self-service withdrawals** — request, review, approve, and pay out, mirroring the deposit flow that already works well.
2. **Build a treasury no single person can move alone.** The end-state design uses a multi-signature treasury requiring several independent approvals — the system key, the treasury manager, and the branch manager together — before any treasury funds move, removing single-person risk entirely from the custody layer. Fred's stewardship of SovLend's treasury account today is the responsible first step toward this, not a workaround to it.
3. **Let clients hold and grow Bitcoin savings directly**, and repay loans in Bitcoin as well as fiat — turning SovLend into a genuine Bitcoin circular economy for its client base, not just a funding channel for investors.
4. **Give cashiers a dedicated, purpose-built dashboard** for the full range of channel choices and approvals they now handle.

None of these are blockers to operating today — they are what makes the system stronger, more automated, and less dependent on any one individual as adoption grows.

## 7. The bottom line for investors and leadership

- Bitcoin is real, live, and moving money today — not a slide deck.
- It sits inside the same accounting discipline SovLend already runs on: balanced, permanent, auditable, approved.
- The controls in place today are intentionally conservative — daily caps, manager sign-off, one accountable treasury manager — while the fully automated, multi-signature version is built out.
- Every step on the roadmap makes the system safer and more independent of any single person, never less.
